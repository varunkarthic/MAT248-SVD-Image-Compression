A Images as matrices

A grey-scale image with m rows and n columns of pixels is a matrix A∈ℝm×n with entries in {0, …, 255}. A colour image has one such matrix per channel: three for RGB, AR,AG,AB, four for RGBA (with transparency). This project decomposes each channel separately.

B The factorisation

A=UΣVT
Dimensions of the factors

The diagonal entries satisfy σ1≥σ2≥…≥σr≥0. They are the square roots of the eigenvalues of ATA, and the number of nonzero ones is the rank.

C Rank-k approximation

Ak=∑i=1kσiuiviT

Each term σiuiviT is a rank-one matrix whose Frobenius norm is σᵢ, and the terms are mutually orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product, so ‖A‖F2=∑iσi2. Large singular values are therefore the layers that matter most for the sum.

D The Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem

Theorem. Let A∈ℝm×n have singular values σ1≥σ2≥… and let k≤rank(A). Among all matrices B∈ℝm×n of rank at most k, the truncated SVD Ak minimises ‖A−B‖ both in the Frobenius norm and in the spectral (2-)norm, and

‖A−Ak‖F=∑i>kσi2,‖A−Ak‖2=σk+1.

For k = rank(A) both errors are zero. Eckart and Young (1936) proved the Frobenius case; Mirsky (1960) extended it to unitarily invariant norms, including the spectral norm. The minimiser is unique in the Frobenius norm when σk>σk+1.

E Energy retention

E(k)=∑i=1kσi2∑iσi2

Because ‖A‖F2=∑iσi2, E(k) is the share of the matrix's squared size kept by the first k layers. It is nondecreasing, between 0 and 1, and equals 1 at k = rank(A). For the all-zero matrix the denominator is 0: the ratio is not applicable, and we avoid dividing by zero (E = 1 is the natural convention, since nothing is lost).

F Storage count

stored scalars =k(m+n+1) per channel

The factors are k columns of U (m·k numbers), k rows of Vᵀ (n·k numbers) and k singular values: k(m + n + 1) numbers for an m×n matrix. An RGB image multiplies this by 3 (one count per channel). For the adaptive block mode, with a tile of size bh × bw and rank kt in tile t, the count per tile is C(1+kt(bh+bw+1)) with C channels.

This is a count of scalars, not a file size. The PNG that the laboratory produces is simply the reconstructed image encoded as PNG. Plain float64 SVD factors can be larger than the 8-bit image itself, and codecs such as JPEG or WebP can beat simple SVD compression. The saving formula only says when k(m+n+1) < mn, that is when k<mnm+n+1.

G Approximation error

  • Frobenius error ‖A−B‖F=∑i,j(aij−bij)2; relative error ‖A−B‖‖A‖(Frobenius norms ).
  • MSE is the mean of the squared pixel differences, MSE=1N‖A−B‖2 with N the number of values.
  • PSNR PSNR=10log10(2552MSE) in dB for 8-bit images; infinite when MSE = 0.
  • SSIM compares local luminance, contrast and structure of two images; it is 1 for identical images.

The spectral error formulas of theorem D hold for the exact floating-point reconstruction. The image you see is rounded to integers and clipped to 0–255, so its measured error can differ a little from the theoretical value; the laboratory reports the measured one and the spectral one separately.

H Exact and randomised SVD

Exact (one-sided Jacobi)

Rotates pairs of columns until all columns are orthogonal; the column norms are then the singular values. It gives the full spectrum with orthogonality-accurate factors. Cost is about O(m n²) per sweep, and a handful of sweeps are needed, so it is limited to moderate image sizes.

Randomised (Halko–Martinsson–Tropp)

  1. Sketch: Y = A Ω with a random n×(k+p) matrix Ω.
  2. Optionally apply a few power iterations to sharpen the spectrum.
  3. Orthonormalise Y by a QR factorisation (Householder) to get Q.
  4. Take the SVD of the small matrix QᵀA and lift it back.

Cost is about O(m n k). It is an approximation: it is not guaranteed to equal the optimal Ak, so the project reports the energy it actually achieves.

Limitations of this project

  • The SVD is a global low-rank model; it does not exploit local structure the way block transform codecs do. Standard formats such as JPEG and WebP can compress photographs better.
  • The storage count is a count of numbers, not bytes; no entropy coding or quantisation of the factors is applied.
  • Colour channels are treated independently, with no colour-space transform.
  • The exact SVD is limited to images of at most 262,144 pixels (512 × 512) in this application; the randomised method is an approximation.
  • Displayed images are rounded to 8 bits, so measured errors can differ slightly from the exact theory.
