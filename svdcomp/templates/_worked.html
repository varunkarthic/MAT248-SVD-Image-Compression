Part 2 · The calculation on the board

A 2×2 example you can do on the board

Everything here is exact and can be done by hand with A=[3113]. Read all twelve steps below, step through them, or switch to board mode to reveal the derivation one line at a time in large type.

All 12 steps shown.

  1. 1 Compute AᵀA

    Multiply A by its transpose. Here A is symmetric, so Aᵀ = A and AᵀA = A². Entry (i, j) is row i of Aᵀ times column j of A.

    A=[3113],AT=[3113]
    ATA=[3·3+1·13·1+1·31·3+3·11·1+3·3]
    ATA=[106610]

  2. 2 Characteristic polynomial and eigenvalues

    The eigenvalues λ of AᵀA are the numbers that make AᵀA − λI singular, that is det(AᵀA − λI) = 0.

    det(ATA−λI)=det[10−λ6610−λ]
    =(10−λ)2−36=λ2−20λ+64=(λ−16)(λ−4)
    λ1=16,λ2=4
    Check: trace=10+10=20=16+4;det=100−36=64=16·4

  3. 3 Eigenvectors

    For each eigenvalue solve (AᵀA − λI)v = 0, then divide by the length so that the vector has length 1.

    λ=16:[−666−6]v=0⇒v1=v2⇒v1=12[11]
    λ=4:[6666]v=0⇒v1=−v2⇒v2=12[1−1]
    ‖(1,1)‖=1+1=2, so we divide by 2
    v1=12[11],v2=12[1−1]

  4. 4 Singular values

    The singular values are the square roots of the eigenvalues of AᵀA, listed from largest to smallest.

    σ1=16=4,σ2=4=2
    σ=(4,2),4≥2≥0

  5. 5 V and U

    The eigenvectors are the columns of V. Each column of U comes from u = Av / σ, which is what makes A = UΣVᵀ work.

    V=[v1v2]=12[111−1]
    VT=12[111−1] (V is symmetric)
    Av1=12[3·1+1·11·1+3·1]=12[44]=4·12[11]=4v1⇒u1=v1
    Av2=12[3·1+1·(−1)1·1+3·(−1)]=12[2−2]=2·12[1−1]=2v2⇒u2=v2
    U=[u1u2]=12[111−1]
    Sign freedom: replacing vi and ui by their negatives together gives the same product; the engine fixes the sign of the first row of U.

  6. 6 Sigma

    Σ is the diagonal matrix of singular values, in descending order. For a square 2×2 matrix it is 2×2.

    Σ=[4002]

  7. 7 Verify: U Σ Vᵀ = A

    Multiply the three matrices back together, two multiplications, one at a time. Both 1/√2 factors are pulled out front, so only whole numbers are multiplied.

    UΣ=12[111−1][4002]=12[424−2]
    (UΣ)VT=12[424−2]12[111−1]=12[6226]
    UΣVT=[3113]=A✓

  8. 8 Rank-1 approximation A₁

    Keep only the strongest layer: A₁ = σ₁ u₁ v₁ᵀ. A column times a row gives a 2×2 matrix of rank 1.

    A1=σ1u1v1T=4·12[11]12[11]=4·12[1111]
    A1=[2222]

  9. 9 The residual and its size

    The residual is what the rank-1 picture got wrong. Its Frobenius norm is the square root of the sum of the squares of all entries.

    A−A1=[3−21−21−23−2]=[1−1−11]
    ‖A−A1‖F=1+1+1+1=2=σ2
    Eckart–Young: ‖A−A1‖F=∑i>1σi2=22=2

  10. 10 Retained spectral energy

    The energy of a matrix is the sum of the squares of its entries, which equals the sum of the squares of its singular values. The share kept by A₁ is the retained energy.

    ‖A‖F2=9+1+1+9=20=42+22=16+4
    E(1)=σ12σ12+σ22=1620=0.8=80%
    The remaining 20 % is σ22=4 out of 20.

  11. 11 The next layer and exact reconstruction

    Add the second rank-one component σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ. Together the two layers give back A exactly.

    σ2u2v2T=2·12[1−1−11]=[1−1−11]
    A1+σ2u2v2T=[2222]+[1−1−11]=[3113]=A
    Rank 2:energy 100%,error 0.

  12. 12 An honest note about storage

    This tiny example teaches the mathematics; it is not a storage saving. Storing the rank-1 factors takes k(m + n + 1) = 1·(2 + 2 + 1) = 5 numbers, more than the 4 numbers of A. Compression pays off only for large matrices whose singular values decay quickly.

    k(m+n+1)=1·(2+2+1)=5>4=mn

What the three factors do to the plane

Take the unit circle and apply VT, then Σ, then U. The orthogonal factors keep lengths and angles (here each is a reflection, determinant −1); Σ stretches the axes by 4 and 2, giving the ellipse. This picture illustrates the factorisation; it is not the algorithm used to compute it.
