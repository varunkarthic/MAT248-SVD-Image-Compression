The scalar count k(m+n+1) is a mathematical count of numbers to store, not a file size. Plain SVD factors kept as 64-bit floats can take more space than the original image, and JPEG or WebP normally compress better than a simple truncated SVD. The spectral error is exact mathematics before rounding; the pixel error is measured after rounding and clipping to 8-bit values.

C Compute the SVD

Before the animation

  1. The image is a matrix A of pixel brightnesses, one matrix per colour channel.
  2. The SVD writes it as A = U Σ VT.
  3. Σ holds the singular values, largest first; the columns of U and V are the singular vectors.

Then reconstruction starts at rank 1 and adds one component at a time.

C Adaptive blocks

Cut the image into tiles, run an exact SVD on every tile, and give each tile its own rank.

Detailed tiles get a higher rank because more of their energy sits in smaller singular values; smooth tiles are almost rank 1. Limits: tile borders can show blocking; each tile stores its mean plus its own factors, storage per tile is C·(1 + kt(bh + bw + 1)); an exact SVD is computed for every tile (slower for small tiles); the ranks are chosen globally from the pooled singular values, not tile by tile.

D One singular component

A single component σi ui viT is signed: it adds light in some places and removes it in others, so it is not itself a displayable photograph. The reconstruction is the sum of the first k of them.
