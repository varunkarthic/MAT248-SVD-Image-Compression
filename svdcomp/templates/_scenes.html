Watch the factorisation

First the matrix is pulled apart into three simple factors, then the factors are read as a sum of ranked layers. Both scenes can be paused, stepped and replayed, and each has a plain 2D version.

Fig. A The SVD factorisation

A=UΣVT
Step 0 of 3: the matrix A.
A m × n
The matrix we start from: for an image, one number per pixel.
U m × m
Rotation or reflection of the output directions (its columns are orthonormal).
Σ m × n, diagonal
Stretch by σ1≥σ2≥…≥0: how strongly each direction is amplified.
VT n × n
Rotation or reflection of the input directions (its rows are orthonormal).

Honest caption: the boxes sliding apart are an illustration of the factorisation, not a computation. Moving boxes is not itself an SVD operation; the numbers come from the algorithm in the laboratory below.

Fig. B Singular components

Every matrix is a sum of rank-one layers: A=σ1u1v1T +σ2u2v2T +…. Keeping the first k layers gives the best rank-k approximation.
